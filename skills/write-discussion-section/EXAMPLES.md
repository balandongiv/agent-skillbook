# Examples: Write Discussion Section with Defensible Claims

## Example 1: Softening causal and novelty overclaims

### Before (without this skill)

```
This proves that pooling epochs causes the threshold to rise, and ours is the first study to solve
blink detection in epoch-structured EEG.
```

### After (with this skill applied)

```
These results are consistent with the hypothesis that pooling blink-free and blink-heavy epochs may
distort threshold estimation, which would reduce detection quality. To our knowledge, this is among the
first studies to explicitly account for epoch structure when estimating the blink-detection threshold.
```

### Why it's better

"Proves/causes" become "consistent with/may distort", and "the first study" becomes "to our knowledge,
among the first" — claims the evidence can actually carry.

## Example 2: A specific limitations paragraph vs a vague one

### Before (without this skill)

```
This study has some limitations and more work is needed in the future.
```

### After (with this skill applied)

```
Several limitations temper interpretation. Evaluation is limited to two driving-EEG corpora, so
generalisation to other paradigms, devices, and populations is untested. The method is single-channel
and amplitude-threshold based, and does not exploit spatial or morphological information. No comparison
against deep-learning detectors is provided, and the ground-truth annotations are dataset-specific.
```

### Why it's better

The limitations are concrete and tied to the actual study scope (datasets, method assumptions, missing
baselines, annotation specificity) rather than a generic disclaimer.
